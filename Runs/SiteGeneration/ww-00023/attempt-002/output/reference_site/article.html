The AI Chip War Nobody Is Talking About

As Nvidia and a new wave of challengers race to own the silicon layer of intelligence, the real battle is happening in board rooms most people have never heard of.

By Jordan Mercer June 12, 2025 11 Min Read

Silicon Is the New Oil

For decades, software was the glamorous layer. Hardware was the plumbing — important, sure, but not the thing that made headlines or minted billionaires overnight. That era is over. The race to build the chips that will power artificial intelligence is now the most consequential industrial competition on the planet, and most of the public has no idea it's happening.

Nvidia's H100 became the must-have commodity of 2023, with data centers paying eye-watering premiums and waiting months for allocations. But while everyone watched Jensen Huang take victory laps in his leather jacket, a quieter war was already underway. AMD, Intel, Google, Amazon, Microsoft, Apple, and a dozen well-funded startups are all betting that the AI chip market is too large — and too strategically critical — to cede to a single supplier.

“Whoever controls the silicon controls the economics of inference, training, and ultimately the cost of intelligence itself.”

The stakes go beyond market share. Whoever controls the silicon controls the economics of inference, training, and ultimately the cost of intelligence itself. Governments have noticed. The CHIPS Act, EU semiconductor initiatives, and China's aggressive domestic push are all symptoms of the same realization: AI supremacy runs through the fab.
